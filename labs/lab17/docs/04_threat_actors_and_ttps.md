# Threat actors and their techniques

Threat actors are the people or groups behind an attack. Nation-state groups, often called advanced persistent threats or APTs, pursue espionage and long-term access and are patient and well funded. Cybercriminal groups want money; ransomware gangs encrypt data and threaten to leak it unless a ransom is paid, a model known as double extortion. Hacktivists act for political or social causes, usually with defacements and denial of service attacks, while insiders misuse access they already have.

MITRE ATT&CK is the common language for describing how actors behave. It organises techniques under tactics such as initial access, execution, persistence, privilege escalation, defence evasion, credential access, lateral movement, command and control and exfiltration. For example, phishing with a malicious attachment is a technique of initial access, and abusing valid accounts can provide both initial access and persistence. Mapping a campaign to ATT&CK lets defenders compare it with earlier incidents, find gaps in their detection coverage and prioritise controls. Because techniques change far more slowly than IP addresses or file hashes, detections built on behaviour remain useful long after the original indicators of compromise have expired.
