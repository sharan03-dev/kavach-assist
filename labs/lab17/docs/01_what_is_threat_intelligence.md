# What is threat intelligence

Threat intelligence is evidence-based knowledge about existing or emerging threats that helps an organisation decide how to defend itself. Raw data such as a list of IP addresses becomes intelligence only after it is collected, processed, analysed and given context: who is behind the activity, what they want, how they operate and how confident the analyst is. Intelligence is usually described at three levels. Strategic intelligence is written for executives and explains trends, risks and the motivation of threat actors in business language. Operational intelligence describes specific campaigns, for example a ransomware group that targets hospitals through exposed VPN appliances. Tactical intelligence is the technical layer used by security tools, such as indicators of compromise and the tactics, techniques and procedures of an attacker.

The intelligence lifecycle has six steps. Direction sets the questions that matter to the organisation. Collection gathers data from open sources, commercial feeds, sharing communities and internal telemetry. Processing normalises and de-duplicates the data. Analysis turns it into judgements. Dissemination delivers the result to the people and tools that need it, and feedback tells the team whether the intelligence was useful. Good intelligence is timely, relevant, accurate and actionable. A feed that arrives a week after an attack, or that cannot be acted upon, has little value even if it is technically correct.
